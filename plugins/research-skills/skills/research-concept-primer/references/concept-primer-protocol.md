# Concept Primer Protocol

## 1. Scope before search

Record the selected meaning, aliases, excluded meanings, audience, intended decision,
depth, and source boundary. A term with materially different meanings cannot share
one silent definition.

## 2. Evidence ledger

For each important statement record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | Stable identifier |
| Status | sourced fact / source-local definition / interpretation / unknown |
| Source | Public citation, supplied artifact, or local document identity |
| Time scope | Stable, dated, or current as of retrieval |
| Confidence | high / medium / low with reason |

Search results and retrieved chunks are leads until their content and identity are
checked.

## 3. Dependency ladder

Explain in this order:

1. prerequisite terms the user must already hold;
2. one-sentence purpose and mental model;
3. technical definition and input/output boundary;
4. mechanism or causal story;
5. assumptions and observable implications;
6. failure cases, limits, and open questions.

Skip a layer only when the user's background makes it redundant.

## 4. Contrast test

Choose the nearest two or three confusing alternatives. Contrast them using the same
axis: objective, unit of analysis, mechanism, data assumption, or output. Avoid
dictionary-style lists that do not help the user decide which term applies.

## 5. Reading path

Use stages rather than a flat bibliography:

- orientation: vocabulary and motivation;
- mechanism: canonical or official technical account;
- boundary: critical, comparative, or limitation-focused source.

Each entry states prerequisite, expected learning, and why it is credible.
