# Abstract structure guide

Load this guide only for an abstract `outline` or structural `draft`.

## Choose the story shape

Select the lightest shape that matches the verified contribution:

1. **Challenge → intervention → evidence → implication** for a direct method or
   empirical contribution.
2. **Challenge → limiting assumption → insight → intervention → evidence** when the
   contribution depends on a non-obvious diagnosis.
3. **Setting → gap → contribution set → joint evidence → implication** when several
   contributions must remain distinct.

Do not force every paper into the same sentence count. Each unit must perform one
function and earn its space.

## Build the abstract map

Record:

- **Context:** the minimum background needed to understand the problem.
- **Gap:** the specific unresolved limitation, without an unsupported priority claim.
- **Intervention:** what was designed, derived, measured, or studied.
- **Mechanism or insight:** why the intervention is plausible, if verified.
- **Evidence:** datasets, comparisons, or findings that are safe to state.
- **Implication:** the supported meaning of the result, not a new claim.

Keep contribution and evidence paired. If a numerical result is unavailable or not
verified, use a qualitative supported statement or mark the missing fact.

## Draft checks

- The opening reaches the actual problem quickly.
- The gap is narrower than the field-level motivation.
- Method detail is limited to what distinguishes the contribution.
- Results preserve metric direction, comparison scope, and uncertainty.
- The final implication does not exceed the supplied evidence.
- Citations appear only when the target venue permits them and the source is verified.
