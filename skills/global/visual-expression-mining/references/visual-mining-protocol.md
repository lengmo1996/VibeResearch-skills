# Visual Mining Protocol

## Source observation

Assign `VIS-*` and record:

- paper/document identity, page, figure/table number, caption, and context;
- source image quality, crop, legend, and readable/unreadable elements;
- observable marks, regions, labels, hierarchy, and sequence;
- inferred purpose separately from visible structure.

## Visual grammar

Decompose on consistent axes:

1. semantic role and audience question;
2. information entities, relations, groups, and comparison dimensions;
3. layout topology and alignment;
4. encoding channels: position, length, shape, color, line, texture, size;
5. annotation and source/evidence trace;
6. reading order and progressive disclosure;
7. density, redundancy, and accessibility risks;
8. failure mode when resized, printed, projected, or detached from caption.

## Pattern abstraction

A `PAT-*` must state invariant topology/logic, variable paper-specific content,
required evidence/data, target claim, unsuitable use, source observations, and at
least one counterexample or boundary. A pattern seen once is `source-local`.

## Reuse decision

- `cite`: source visual may be shown under the intended citation/use policy.
- `adapt-structure`: reuse only abstract topology/logic with original content/design.
- `redraw`: reconstruct from verified data/evidence with a new visual expression.
- `permission-required`: intended direct use needs rights confirmation.
- `do-not-use`: rights, fidelity, or evidence risk prevents use.

This is a conservative workflow aid, not legal advice.
